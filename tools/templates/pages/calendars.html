Session deadlines & who represents you

Looking for votes? The Legislation section has bills, roll calls and each legislator's record at the federal, state and county levels.

Knowing who represents your district and when key decisions are being made is essential to effective advocacy. Use the resources below to find your state and federal representatives, follow legislative calendars, and identify the right time to engage. All links lead to official government sources.

Congress · where the two chambers differ

The House and Senate set their calendars separately, and in 2026 they diverge more than they line up. Below is what is still ahead this year.

  • Back from summer

    Aug 10 – Sep 14
    House

    Away most of August; returns Aug 31 for one week, then out again.

    Senate

    Out the whole stretch; returns Sep 14.

    Early September is a very short window if you need both chambers in the room.

  • Pre-election break

    Oct 1 – Nov 9
    House

    Last votes Oct 1, then more than five weeks out.

    Senate

    Out Oct 5 – Nov 6.

    Both chambers clear the calendar to campaign. Election Day is Nov 3. Both return Mon Nov 9, which puts the Senate back six days after the election, not the day after.

  • Veterans Day week

    Nov 9 – Nov 13
    House unconfirmed

    Reported in session the week of Nov 9, Veterans Day included.

    Senate

    Out Nov 11–13.

    The Senate takes the holiday. The House side is from reporting on the calendar, not an official list.

  • Thanksgiving

    Nov 23 – 27
    House

    Out.

    Senate

    Out.

    No difference. Both chambers are away.

  • Year end

    Dec 21 – 31
    House

    No published target adjournment date.

    Senate

    Out Dec 21–31; no published target adjournment date either.

    Neither chamber has published a sine die target for 2026. Year-end dates historically slip.

Earlier this year

  • Jan 30The stopgap funding deadline was missed. Funding lapsed for four days, Jan 31 – Feb 3. A second, DHS-only lapse began Feb 14 and ran to Apr 30.
  • Mar 30 – Apr 10Both chambers were out; both adjourned to Apr 13.
  • Jun 29 – Jul 10The Senate was out both weeks. The House was in session Jun 29, working the run-up to July 4.

Weeks both chambers are away

  • Feb 16 – 20Presidents' Day week
  • May 25 – 29Memorial Day week
  • Nov 23 – 27Thanksgiving week

Senate, in full. The Senate publishes the weeks it will not sit: Jan 1–2, Jan 19–23, Feb 16–20, Mar 30 – Apr 10, May 4–8, May 25–29, Jun 19, Jun 29 – Jul 10, Aug 10 – Sep 11, Oct 5 – Nov 6, Nov 11–13, Nov 23–27, Dec 21–31. Everything else is a session week. It still holds brief pro forma sessions inside those periods, and it has not published a target adjournment date for 2026.
On the House column. The House publishes day-level detail only as a colour-coded PDF, and reissued that calendar in March 2026, so House entries here follow reporting on it rather than an official machine-readable list, and anything I could not confirm is marked. Both official calendars are linked under Federal · calendars above; check them before you book travel.
